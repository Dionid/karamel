# Frame budget and anti-slop

A slide deck is read by someone half-listening to a speaker, not by someone
reading a report alone. That single difference drives every rule below: the
audience gets five seconds per slide, and whatever does not fit in five
seconds belongs in the speaker's notes, not on the screen.

## Frame budget — hard, not "nice to have"

- One slide = one idea. Doesn't fit → that's two slides.
- Heading ≤ 12 words. Subtitle/caption ≤ 25 words. Never more than two short
  paragraphs on one frame.
- Exactly one number gets emphasis per frame. A second emphasis kills the
  first.
- A bullet list is at most 3 items, each ≤ 8 words. Need five bullets? That's
  a table, or two slides.
- A table on a slide is only justified when the table itself is the message
  (a before/after comparison, a competitor matrix). Max 4 columns, 6 rows.
- Empty space is part of the layout, not a mistake. A frame filled to 100%
  does not read.
- If a detail matters but does not fit on the frame, that is never a reason
  to shrink the font — carry it in the speaker notes, or link to a fuller
  report at the end of the deck.
- Speaker notes: 2-5 full sentences per slide, carrying the numbers and
  caveats that were cut from the frame itself. A slide with no notes is a
  slide that got too much text left on it instead.

## Anti-slop — what does not belong on a slide

- Purple gradient on white, "glass" cards, glowing outlines.
- A row of three or four icons with generic one-word captions under them
  ("Reliable", "Fast", "Easy") — the single most recognizable sign of
  generated content. The row itself is not banned; its *empty* version is.
  The fix is a concrete fact with a number and a name under each icon, not
  an adjective.
- Stock illustrations and abstract 3D blobs. Use a real product screenshot,
  a generated image built from an actual reference, or nothing.
- Everything centered: centered heading + centered body + centered image,
  slide after slide.
- Bullets made of nouns with no verbs; arrow/plus/equals glyphs standing in
  for prose (`→ + = ~`).
- The identical layout on every slide in a row — the eye stops
  distinguishing frames.
- Emoji.
- Any automatic animation that runs forever and cannot be paused. Motion
  that starts on its own gets a pause control, or is triggered by a click —
  a frame that keeps moving on its own is a bug, not an effect.

## Numbers on a slide

Only a number the presenter could name a source for. Dates are absolute
("September 14", not "recently"). Thousand separators. The unit goes in the
caption once, not repeated in every cell. A forecast is labelled as a
forecast right on the frame, not left to context.
